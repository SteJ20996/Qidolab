That little moment of discovery

5 liters. You made “almost” exactly right.

Even without marks, the difference between jugs can measure for you.

The useful quantity is how much room remains. Use the empty space in one jug to measure water in the other, and you can leave an amount that was never marked.

Pour a full 7-liter jug into the 4-liter jug and 3 liters remain. Empty the small jug, then move those 3 liters into it. It now has room for exactly 1 more liter. That 1-liter gap becomes another measuring tool.

What if the jugs held 8 and 4 liters? Could filling, emptying, and pouring still produce 5 liters?

No. Starting with empty jugs, all three operations keep each amount a multiple of 4, so 5 is unreachable. This is a powerful approach: look not just for the next move, but for a property every move preserves.

Light-switch experimentOn / off. Just two states.

Tap a light to switch it and its two neighbors: on becomes off, and off becomes on. Lights 1 and 6 are neighbors too.

When the lights went out, a pattern lit up.

Pressing a light twice cancels out. The order of presses does not matter either. You can summarize a whole sequence by which lights were pressed an odd number of times.

You can always undo. Make the chain reaction your tool.
